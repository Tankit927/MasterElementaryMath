A deep narrow valley with steep sides, formed by a river cutting through rock over time, similar to a canyon but often smaller, characterized by river erosion, geological uplift, waterfalls
Gorge
A deep, steep-sided valley carved by a river over geological time through erosion, often with a river at the bottom and can be distinguished from narrower gorges by its large size
Canyon
A deep, winding river bend cut into bedrock, formed when a meandering river's course is uplifted or its base level drops, causing it to rapidly downcut vertically into the land rather than shifting sideways
Entrenched Meander
A series of regular sinuous curves in the channel of a river
Meander
A low-lying, fertile landform shaped like a triangle or fan, created at a river's mouth where it flows into a slower body of water and drops its carried sediments
Delta
Natural cylindrical depressions carved into riverbeds by fluvial erosion from swirling water and trapped rocks, common in upper river courses with turbulent flow
Potholes
A deep basin carved into a streambed at the base of a waterfall by the emmense erosional force of falling water, carrying sand and rocks that scour the bedrock, forming a significant pothole
Plunge pool
Step-like flat platforms on valley sides, representing former floodplains left behind as a river cuts downwards due to uplift or climate shifts, creating staircase of old surfaces
River terraces
A fan or cone shaped deposit of sediment left by a stream as it flows out of a steep mountain valley onto a flatter plain, losing energy and dropping its load
Alluvial fan
A natural or artificial raised bank alongside a river or coast, formed from deposit sediment or built by humans, to prevent flooding and control water flow
Levee
A river system that splits into multiple, interconnected, smaller channels separated by temporary islands of sand and gravel
Braided channel
A bowl-shaped, aphitheater-like basin carved into a mountain side by glacial erosion, formed at the head of a glacial valley
Crique
A sharp, narrow, knife-edged mountain ridge formed by glacial erosion, separating two criques that erode backwards towards each other
Arete/ridge
A sharp pointed mountain peak formed by multiple glaciers eroding a mountain from different sides
Horn
A glacial landform where a smaller tributary valley, carved by a less powerful glacier, ends abruptly high above a larger, deeper valley carved by a massive trunk glacier
Hanging valley
Any accumulation of unconsolidated debris, sometimes referred to as glacial till, that occurs in both currently and formerly glaciated regions, and that has been previously carried along by a glacier or ice sheet
Moraines
Long, winding ridges of sand and gravel deposited by meltwater streams flowing within, on top of, or beneath glaciers. When the glacier melts and retreats, these deposits are left as distinct, snake like landforms, often resembling railway embankments
Eskers
Elongated teardrop-shaped hills made of glacial till, formed beneath moving glaciers and aligned with ice flow, featuring a steep, blunt side (stoss) facing the glacier's origin and a gentle, tapered side (lee) pointing downstream, often found in swarms called "basket of eggs" topography, revealing past glacial movement
Drumlins
A broad, flat, gently sloping area of sorted sand and gravel deposited by meltwater streams flowing from a glacier's terminus
Outwash plains
A depression formed by the collapse or sinking of the ground surface, where water dissolves soluble bedrock like limestone, creating underground voids that can no longer support the soil above, leading to sudden or gradual collapses, often filled with water
Sink holes/dolines
Distinctive, rough, weathered surfaces of limestone, characterized by sharp ridges, pinnacles and deep grooves formed by the dissolution of soluble rock by water
Lapies
A large, compound karst depression formed by the merging or coalescence of several smaller sink holes (dolines) due to ongoing dissolution and subsidence in soluble rocks like limestone, creating elongated or irregular hollows larger than single sink holes
Uvala
A steep, isolated column of rock standing in the sea, formed by coastal erosion from waves attacking weaker rock, eventually creating caves, then arches, which collapse leaving the pillar behind
Stack
A natural, bridge-like rock formation with an opening formed by coastal erosion
Arch
A vast, gently sloping, low-relief plain formed in arid/semi-arid regions by the joining (coalescence) of several pediments, representing a late stage of landscape erosion where mountains are worn down into flat, bedrock surfaces covered by thin sediment
Pediplain
A flat, dry lake bed in an arid basin where water temporarily collects after rain, quickly evaporating or seeping away, leaving behind fine sediments like silt, clay and salts, creating one of the flattest landforms on Earth also called slat flat, pan or dry lake
Playa
India's largest inland salt water lake or playa and its location
Sambhar lake, Rajasthan
World's largest salt water lake or playa and its location
Salar De Uyuni, Bolivia
